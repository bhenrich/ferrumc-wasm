# FerrumC microbenchmarks — 2026-06-29 (`10f58ff3`)

**Characterization microbenchmarks of internal FerrumC paths — NOT a cross-server
comparison; measured on a dev machine.**

These numbers describe the throughput of individual server subsystems on synthetic
data (chunk generation/encode, paletted-section encode, block-placement
resolution, item-slot encode/decode, synthetic simulation ticks). They are **not**
capacity, player-count, or "X players supported" claims, and they are **not** a
comparison against any other server (Paper, Pumpkin, Folia, vanilla, etc.). The
harness never connects to a running server or a socket — it drives the public APIs
of the measured crates in-process.

Treat every figure as ballpark characterization for spotting regressions and
relative cost between code paths, not as a precise or competitive benchmark.

### Measurement parameters (full run, default config)

- **Warmup:** 50 untimed iterations per benchmark.
- **Timed iterations:** 500 per benchmark.
- **Sim edit sweep:** `K ∈ {0, 1, 16, 64, 256}` block edits per synthetic tick.
- **Timing:** `std::time::Instant` around only the region under test;
  `std::hint::black_box` on inputs/outputs to prevent the optimizer eliding work.
- **Stats:** mean / min / max plus nearest-rank `p50` / `p95` / `p99` over the
  sorted per-iteration nanoseconds; throughput = total work units ÷ total measured
  time.

### What each benchmark exercises

- **`world`** — flat chunk generation, whole-chunk network encode (bytes/sec), and
  paletted `ChunkSection` encode for the single-valued (all-air) and indirect
  (palette-indexed) representations.
- **`placement`** — `compute_placement` throughput per block family (simple cube,
  axis log, slab, stairs, floor/wall torch, fence, horizontal-facing) plus an
  all-families mix.
- **`items`** — trusted slot encode (empty / present / present-with-component), the
  container-content payload encode, and untrusted slot decode + validation.
- **`sim`** — `SimShard::run_tick` cost as the per-tick block-edit count grows,
  reported as ns/tick and edits/sec. An `InMemoryStore` backs the chunk acquire
  (storage miss → generate).

### Caveats — read these before quoting any number

1. **Dev machine, not an isolated rig.** Measured on a daily-driver laptop with no
   CPU pinning, no isolated cores, no fixed clocks, and macOS scheduler/turbo
   behavior in play. A background FerrumC dev-server instance and other normal
   desktop processes may have been running during capture. Expect run-to-run
   variance; do not treat these as reproducible-to-the-nanosecond.
2. **Timer granularity floor.** On this host the monotonic clock quantizes to
   roughly ~41.7 ns steps (Apple Silicon mach timebase). Every benchmark whose mean
   is under ~50 ns (most of `items`, the fastest `placement` families,
   `section_encode_single_air`, `sim_tick_k0/k1`) is at or below reliable timer
   resolution — note the `p50 = 0.0 ns` and the 42 ns quantization in those rows.
   Read those as "too fast to time precisely," not as exact figures. Their derived
   throughput (Mops/s, Mbytes/s) inherits the same imprecision.
3. **Synthetic inputs.** Chunks, placements, slots, and ticks are constructed
   in-process, not replayed from real client traffic. They characterize code-path
   cost, not end-to-end server behavior under a real join/movement/edit workload.
4. **Single-threaded microbenchmarks.** Each benchmark runs one path on one thread.
   Nothing here measures shard parallelism, scheduling overhead, contention, or I/O.

## Headline numbers

A few representative figures (see full tables below; all subject to the caveats above):

- **Flat chunk generation:** ~146 µs mean per chunk (~6.85 k chunks/s on one thread).
- **Whole-chunk network encode:** ~832 ns mean (~16.5 KiB/chunk → ~19.9 GB/s).
- **Placement, all-families mix:** ~1.11 µs mean (~7.19 M placements/s).
- **Synthetic tick @ 256 edits:** ~4.52 µs mean (~0.005 mean mspt; ~56.6 M edits/s),
  i.e. a single shard resolves 256 block edits/tick far under the 50 ms tick budget.
